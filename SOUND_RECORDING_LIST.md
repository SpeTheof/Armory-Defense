## HOW IT WORKS

Record each sound as a separate `.mp3` file and drop it into the matching folder.
The game automatically detects and loads them, **replacing the synthesized placeholders**.

**Folder structure:**
```
audio/
├── weapons/     ← Gun firing sounds (one per weapon)
│   ├── glock.mp3
│   ├── desert_eagle.mp3
│   ├── mp5.mp3
│   ├── ... (15 files)
└── sfx/         ← Impacts, UI, explosions
    ├── explosion_small.mp3
    ├── hit_flesh.mp3
    ├── ... (16 files)
```

**Recording tips:**
- Record at 44100Hz, mono is fine
- Keep files SHORT (under 1 second for most, under 2 for big ones)
- Leave a tiny bit of silence at the END (helps with rapid-fire playback)
- For rapid-fire weapons (MP5, UZI, Minigun), record ONE shot — the game repeats it
- Get CLOSE to the mic for punchy sounds
- Do multiple takes, pick the best!

## 🎤 RECORDING ORDER (Suggested)

Start with the easy ones to build confidence:
1. UI sounds (22-27) — easiest, most natural
2. Explosions (16-19) — fun to do!
3. Sidearms (1-2) — simple single sounds
4. SMGs (3-4) — quick and short
5. Assault Rifles (7-8) — similar to SMGs but more body
6. Shotguns (5-6) — need more power
7. Snipers (9-10) — BIG sounds, take your time
8. Heavy (11-13) — RPG is fun, minigun is tricky (keep it tiny!)
9. Experimental (14-15) — most creative freedom
10. Enemy sounds (28-29) — have fun with it!
11. End screens (30-31) — dramatic!
12. Music — Spe's domain 🎵

## ⚡ TIPS FOR RAPID-FIRE WEAPONS (MP5, UZI, Minigun)

These weapons fire VERY fast in game (8-40 shots per second). The game loops your single recording.

**Key tips:**
- Record ONE shot only
- Make it VERY short (under 0.1s)
- Keep it consistent — no variation
- Test by saying "t-t-t-t-t-t-t" rapidly — THAT's what it should sound like individually
- The Minigun needs to be the SHORTEST and most mechanical sounding
